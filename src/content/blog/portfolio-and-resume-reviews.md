A written comment on a resume or portfolio tells you *what* someone thinks. Watching their reaction as they read it — where they pause, what they scroll past quickly, what makes them ask a question — tells you a lot more.

## Why async feedback only gets you so far

Sending a PDF or a portfolio link and waiting for comments back is convenient, but it strips out all the in-the-moment reactions that are often the most useful part of a review: the confused pause on a vague bullet point, the "wait, go back" on a project they want to hear more about.

## How QuiiCast fits

Share your resume, portfolio site, or design file from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and send your mentor, career coach, or friend the 4-digit code. They watch on [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) and can react out loud as they go, while you talk it through together over a call — pausing on the sections that need work instead of guessing which ones from a list of written notes.

It also works the other way: if a mentor wants to show you *their* resume or portfolio as a reference, they can share their screen just as easily.

## Good moments for this

- A mock interview where the interviewer reviews your resume live before the questions start
- Getting a second pair of eyes on a portfolio site before you send it out
- A career coach walking through what recruiters actually look at first, on a real example
- Comparing two versions of a resume side by side while talking through the differences

It's a small, low-friction way to turn a document review into an actual conversation — no uploading anything, no account, just a code and a call.
