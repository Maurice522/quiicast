A written comment on a resume or portfolio tells you *what* someone thinks. Watching their reaction as they read it — where they pause, what they scroll past quickly, what makes them ask a question — tells you a lot more, and it happens in the moment instead of days later in a document's margin.

## Why async feedback only gets you so far

Sending a PDF or a portfolio link and waiting for comments back is convenient, but it strips out all the in-the-moment reactions that are often the most useful part of a review: the confused pause on a vague bullet point, the "wait, go back" on a project they want to hear more about, the moment their eyes glaze over on a paragraph that's clearly too dense.

## Running a live review

1. Open your resume, portfolio site, or design file on your screen, scrolled to the top.
2. Go to [quiicast.com/caster](/caster), start sharing, and send your mentor, career coach, or friend the 4-digit code.
3. They open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) and watch as you scroll through, narrating and pausing on the sections that matter most.
4. Talk it through together over a call while they react out loud — pausing on the sections that need work instead of guessing which ones from a list of written notes afterward.

It also works the other way: if a mentor wants to show you *their* resume or portfolio as a reference, they can share their screen just as easily, which is a genuinely useful way to see what "good" looks like in your specific field.

## Good moments for this

- A mock interview where the interviewer reviews your resume live before the questions start
- Getting a second pair of eyes on a portfolio site before you send it out
- A career coach walking through what recruiters actually look at first, on a real example
- Comparing two versions of a resume side by side while talking through the differences
- A quick gut-check before submitting an application, when you just need someone to skim it in real time

## Getting more out of the session

Ask whoever's reviewing to think out loud, not just at the end but as they go — the "wait, what does this mean" reaction in the middle of reading a bullet point is far more useful feedback than a polished summary five minutes later, because it's their actual, unfiltered first impression.

## FAQ

**Do I need to send them the file beforehand?**
No — since they're watching your screen live, there's nothing to send or upload ahead of time. You're the one controlling what's on screen and when.

**Can they highlight or mark up my resume directly?**
Not through QuiiCast itself — it's view-only. If you want actual markup, you'd still want to send the file separately afterward for them to comment on, using whatever a session raised as talking points.

**Is this better than just sharing a Google Doc with comments enabled?**
They serve different purposes — comments are great for detailed line-by-line notes you can revisit later, while a live session is better for capturing immediate reactions and asking follow-up questions on the spot. Many people find doing both, in that order, works best.

It's a small, low-friction way to turn a document review into an actual conversation — no uploading anything, no account, just a code and a call.
