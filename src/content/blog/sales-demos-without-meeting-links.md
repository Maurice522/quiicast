Some product demos are scheduled a week out with a deck and a calendar invite. Others happen because a prospect messaged you mid-conversation asking to actually see the thing you're describing — and the moment usually passes if you make them wait for a Zoom link and a meeting to spin up.

## Why an ad hoc demo needs less ceremony

A full video-conferencing tool is the right call for a formal sales call with multiple stakeholders. It's a lot of setup for "let me quickly show you what I mean" — especially when the prospect is on their phone and doesn't want to download an app just to watch you click around a dashboard for two minutes. Every extra step between "can you show me" and actually showing them is a chance for the moment to pass and the conversation to go cold.

## Running the demo in the moment

1. Get your product open to the right screen — a dashboard, a specific feature, whatever the prospect asked about.
2. Go to [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and start sharing.
3. Send the 4-digit code over whatever channel you're already talking in — email, LinkedIn, a chat widget.
4. The prospect opens [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on any device, types the code, and is watching your screen in seconds.

No account, no app store, no "sorry, one sec, installing." Since up to 5 people can join the same code, this works just as well for a small buying committee watching together as it does for a single prospect.

## Good moments to use it

- Someone asks "can you show me" in the middle of a chat conversation
- A support conversation turns into "actually, let me just show you how this works"
- A quick internal demo to a colleague who wants to see progress before a bigger review
- Following up on a cold email with "here's a 2-minute look" instead of a recorded video
- A prospect on a call asks a question that's much faster to answer by showing than explaining

## Keeping it tight

Since there's no formal meeting structure holding the demo together, it helps to say up front what you're about to show and how long it'll take — "let me pull up the reporting dashboard, this'll take two minutes" — so the prospect knows what to expect and stays engaged instead of wondering where the conversation is headed.

## FAQ

**Does the prospect need to download anything?**
No — they just open a link in whatever browser they already have and type a 4-digit code.

**Can I record the demo for later?**
Not through QuiiCast itself — it doesn't record or store anything. If you want a recording for your own reference or for a prospect who couldn't attend live, you'd need separate screen-recording software running locally on your side.

**Is this appropriate for a formal, multi-stakeholder pitch?**
It's built for the informal, spontaneous case — for a scheduled call with slides, stakeholder introductions, and a structured agenda, your usual video-conferencing platform is still the better fit.

It's deliberately not built for a formal, recorded, multi-slide sales presentation — for that, your usual video platform is still the right tool. This is for the demos that would otherwise not happen at all because setting one up felt like too much friction for the moment.
