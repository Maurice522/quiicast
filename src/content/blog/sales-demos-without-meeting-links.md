Some product demos are scheduled a week out with a deck and a calendar invite. Others happen because a prospect messaged you mid-conversation asking to actually see the thing you're describing — and the moment usually passes if you make them wait for a Zoom link and a meeting to spin up.

## Why an ad hoc demo needs less ceremony

A full video-conferencing tool is the right call for a formal sales call with multiple stakeholders. It's a lot of setup for "let me quickly show you what I mean" — especially when the prospect is on their phone and doesn't want to download an app just to watch you click around a dashboard for two minutes.

## How QuiiCast fits

Start sharing from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and send the 4-digit code over whatever channel you're already talking in — email, LinkedIn, a chat widget. The prospect opens [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on any device, types the code, and is watching your screen in seconds. No account, no app store, no "sorry, one sec, installing."

Since up to 5 people can join the same code, this works just as well for a small buying committee watching together as it does for a single prospect.

## Good moments to use it

- Someone asks "can you show me" in the middle of a chat conversation
- A support conversation turns into "actually, let me just show you how this works"
- A quick internal demo to a colleague who wants to see progress before a bigger review
- Following up on a cold email with "here's a 2-minute look" instead of a recorded video

It's deliberately not built for a formal, recorded, multi-slide sales presentation — for that, your usual video platform is still the right tool. This is for the demos that would otherwise not happen at all because setting one up felt like too much friction for the moment.
