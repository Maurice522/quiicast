Study groups often revolve around whoever has the best notes, the clearest summary, or the practice problem set everyone actually needs. Getting everyone looking at the same material at the same time is half the battle — especially when the group isn't all in the same room.

## Group chats aren't built for "watch this with me"

Sending a photo of notes or a link to a shared doc works, but it's static — someone has to describe what they're pointing at, and it's easy for the group to end up looking at slightly different things. What actually helps is everyone seeing the same screen, live, while whoever's presenting talks through it.

## How QuiiCast fits

Whoever has the material shares their screen from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and drops the 4-digit code in the group chat. Everyone else opens [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on their own laptop or phone and follows along — up to 5 people on a single code, which covers most study groups without anyone needing to sign up for anything.

Because there's no account needed on either side, it's genuinely as fast as sending a message and having everyone join within a minute — useful for a study session that comes together spontaneously the night before an exam.

## Good ways to use it

- One person walks through practice problems while everyone follows along and asks questions
- Reviewing shared class notes together instead of everyone squinting at a photo
- Going through a professor's slides section by section as a group
- A quick last-minute review session that doesn't need to be scheduled in advance

It's casual by design — there's no recording, no persistent classroom, nothing to set up beforehand. Just a code, and everyone looking at the same screen.
