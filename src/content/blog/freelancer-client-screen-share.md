Showing a client work in progress usually means one of two things: setting up a staging environment they can log into, or sending a video recording that's already out of date by the time they watch it. Sometimes what you actually want is simpler — just show them, live, right now, and answer their questions as they come up instead of collecting them in a follow-up email.

## The overhead of "let me set up access"

Giving a client staging credentials, a shared design-tool seat, or a preview link with the right permissions takes setup — reasonable for an ongoing engagement, but a lot of friction for "can I quickly show you where this is at." It's worse when the tool you're working in (Figma, VS Code, a local dev environment, an editing suite) doesn't have a clean way to invite an outside viewer at all.

## How to run the call

1. Get the work open exactly how you want the client to see it — the right file, the right frame, the right scroll position.
2. Go to [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and start sharing. Pick the specific application window rather than your full screen if you have other client files or messages open elsewhere.
3. Send the client the 4-digit code by email, text, or whatever channel you already use with them.
4. They open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) — no login to your design tool, no staging credentials, no account — and watch you walk through it live, asking questions as they go.

Because it's your own screen, you're always showing exactly the state you intend to — no risk of a client poking around a staging environment and seeing something unfinished you didn't mean to show yet.

## Where this fits a freelance workflow

- A quick progress check-in without a formal deliverable ready to send
- Walking through a design file, codebase, or campaign dashboard live
- Getting immediate reactions and feedback instead of waiting on written comments
- Presenting a final result before sending over the actual files or access
- Showing a client exactly how to use a deliverable you just handed off, without writing a separate instructions document

## Coming across as professional

Clients notice small things. Close browser tabs with other clients' names in them, mute notifications before you start, and pick the specific window you're sharing (most browsers let you choose a single app window or tab instead of your entire screen) so a stray Slack popup or personal tab never shows up mid-call.

## FAQ

**Can I share just my design tool and not my whole desktop?**
Yes — when your browser asks what to share, choose the specific window or tab instead of "Entire Screen." That keeps everything else on your desktop private by default.

**Does the client need to install anything?**
No. They open a link, type a 4-digit code, and watch — no app, no browser extension, no account.

**What if the client is on their phone?**
The receiver page works on any modern mobile browser, so a client checking in from their phone between meetings can still watch without issue.

For long-term collaboration, proper shared access is still worth setting up — this is for the informal "let me show you where we're at" moments that happen between milestones, where setting up formal access would be more work than the update itself.
