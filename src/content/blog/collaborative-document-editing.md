Shared documents are great for editing together asynchronously. But some conversations need more than two cursors in the same file — you need to actually point at a specific cell, a specific paragraph, and talk through it in real time before either of you touches anything.

## When "share the link" isn't quite right

Sending a document link works fine when the other person just needs to read or edit it on their own time. It's a worse fit when you need to walk them through *why* a formula is built a certain way, or explain a section before they start editing — especially if the file has permissions, formatting, or context that's easier to show than describe.

## How QuiiCast fits

Share your screen from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) with the document already open, and send the 4-digit code to whoever needs to follow along on [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver). They see exactly what's on your screen — your cursor, your formatting, your comments — without needing edit access, a shared drive invite, or an account on whatever platform the document lives in.

This is particularly useful when the document itself is sensitive or lives somewhere you don't want to grant broader access to — you're showing it, not sharing a link that could be forwarded.

## Where this comes in handy

- Walking a colleague through a budget spreadsheet before handing off editing
- Reviewing a contract or proposal live with someone who shouldn't get direct edit access
- Explaining a complex formula or pivot table by showing it in action
- Onboarding someone to a working document without adding them as a permanent collaborator yet

Once they understand the file, you can always follow up with a proper shared link for actual collaborative editing — this is for the conversation that needs to happen first.
