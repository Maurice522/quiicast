Shared documents are great for editing together asynchronously. But some conversations need more than two cursors in the same file — you need to actually point at a specific cell, a specific paragraph, and talk through it in real time before either of you touches anything.

## When "share the link" isn't quite right

Sending a document link works fine when the other person just needs to read or edit it on their own time. It's a worse fit when you need to walk them through *why* a formula is built a certain way, explain a section before they start editing, or the file has permissions, formatting, or context that's easier to show than describe. It's also awkward when the document lives somewhere the other person doesn't have — or shouldn't have — an account: a different company's Google Workspace, an internal Notion, an accounting tool with a per-seat license.

## Walking through it, step by step

1. Open the document, spreadsheet, or file on your own machine and get it to the exact view you want to show.
2. Go to [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and click **Start sharing my screen** — choose the specific window or tab with the document, not your whole desktop, so nothing else on your screen is visible.
3. Send the 4-digit code to your colleague however you're already talking — chat, email, a quick call.
4. They open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver), type the code, and see your screen — your cursor, your formatting, your comments — without needing edit access, a shared drive invite, or an account on whatever platform the document lives in.

This is particularly useful when the document itself is sensitive or lives somewhere you don't want to grant broader access to — you're showing it, not sharing a link that could be forwarded, downloaded, or copied elsewhere.

## Where this comes in handy

- Walking a colleague through a budget spreadsheet before handing off editing
- Reviewing a contract or proposal live with someone who shouldn't get direct edit access
- Explaining a complex formula, macro, or pivot table by showing it in action rather than describing the logic
- Onboarding someone to a working document without adding them as a permanent collaborator yet
- Cross-company reviews, where the other side's IT department would never approve adding an external collaborator to an internal spreadsheet, but watching one is fine

## A privacy tip worth taking seriously

Before you share, close anything you wouldn't want your colleague to see — other browser tabs, notification popups, a messaging app in the background. Sharing a single window or tab (rather than your entire screen) is the safest option if your browser's share picker offers it, since it keeps everything else on your desktop out of view entirely.

## FAQ

**Can they zoom in on their end without messing up my view?**
Yes — they're watching a video of your screen, so anything they do on their own device (zooming, taking a screenshot, resizing the window) has zero effect on what you see or how you're sharing.

**What if I need them to actually edit something?**
QuiiCast is one-way viewing, not remote control or co-editing — once they understand the file, follow up with a proper shared link (Google Docs sharing, a spreadsheet invite, whatever the platform uses) for the actual collaborative editing.

**Is the connection private?**
The video goes directly between your two browsers (peer-to-peer), and the 4-digit code is only valid for a short window and stops working once you stop sharing.

Once they understand the file, you can always follow up with a proper shared link for actual collaborative editing — this is for the conversation that needs to happen first.
