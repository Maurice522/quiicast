Sometimes you just want to show someone something on your screen — a video that's too good to describe, a photo album from a trip, a strange listing you found while house-hunting — and they're not in the room with you.

## The gap between "send a link" and "watch it together"

Sending a link works, but it's not the same as watching together: reacting in real time, pausing to point something out, narrating what's coming up. Dedicated watch-party tools exist, but they usually mean both people signing into the same streaming service, which doesn't help for a photo album, a random browser tab, or a video that isn't on a platform with watch-together built in at all.

## Sharing whatever's on your screen

1. Get whatever you want to show — a video player, a folder of photos, a webpage — open and ready.
2. Go to [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and click **Start sharing my screen**.
3. Send the other person the 4-digit code over text, chat, or while you're already on the phone.
4. They open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on their laptop, tablet, or phone and see exactly what you see, in real time.

Because it's peer-to-peer and works in any browser, it doesn't matter what app the content lives in or what device the other person has — a photo album in one person's phone gallery synced to a laptop, a video on a niche site with no watch-together feature, a listing on a real estate site, all work the same way.

## A few practical notes

- This is genuinely casual, one-way sharing — there's no synced playback control on their end, so it works best with a bit of narration ("okay, pausing here").
- Audio from your screen is included in the share when you choose to share it in your browser's picker, so a video with sound comes through — just make sure the person watching isn't muted on their end.
- If either of you is on mobile data, dropping to a lower quality preset keeps things smooth without eating through a data plan, especially for something like a slow scroll through photos where motion isn't the point.
- If you're sharing your whole desktop rather than one window, close anything you wouldn't want the other person glancing at, since it'll all be visible.

## FAQ

**Can the other person pause or control playback?**
No — they're watching a live video feed of your screen, so all playback control stays on your end. If you pause the video you're sharing, they see it pause too, just with no way to trigger that themselves.

**Will there be a noticeable delay?**
Typically it's small enough that talking over the phone or a call still feels in sync — enough to comfortably say "look at this part" and have them see it moments later, not seconds behind.

**What's the best way to talk while sharing?**
QuiiCast handles the video only, so keep a phone call or video-call app running alongside it for the actual conversation — narrating what you're doing as you go makes the one-way nature of the sharing feel much more like watching together.

It won't replace a dedicated watch-party app for regularly watching shows together — but for "you have to see this," it's the fastest way to actually show someone, not just tell them.
