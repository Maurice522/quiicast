Sometimes you just want to show someone something on your screen — a video that's too good to describe, a photo album from a trip, a strange listing you found while house-hunting — and they're not in the room with you.

## The gap between "send a link" and "watch it together"

Sending a link works, but it's not the same as watching together: reacting in real time, pausing to point something out, narrating what's coming up. Dedicated watch-party tools exist, but they usually mean both people signing into the same streaming service, which doesn't help for a photo album, a random browser tab, or a video that isn't on a platform with watch-together built in.

## How QuiiCast fits

Whatever's on your screen — a video player, a folder of photos, a webpage — share it from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) and send the other person the 4-digit code. They open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on their laptop, tablet, or phone and see exactly what you see, in real time, while you talk over the phone or a call.

Because it's peer-to-peer and works in any browser, it doesn't matter what app the content lives in or what device the other person has.

## A few practical notes

- This is genuinely casual, one-way sharing — there's no synced playback control on their end, so it works best with a bit of narration ("okay, pausing here").
- Audio from your screen is included in the share, so a video with sound comes through — just make sure the person watching isn't muted on their end.
- If either of you is on mobile data, dropping to a lower quality preset keeps things smooth without eating through a data plan.

It won't replace a dedicated watch-party app for regularly watching shows together — but for "you have to see this," it's the fastest way to actually show someone, not just tell them.
