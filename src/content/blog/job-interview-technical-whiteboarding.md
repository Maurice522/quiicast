Live-coding interview platforms are useful when you want a shared, sandboxed editor. But sometimes what you actually want to see is how a candidate works in their *own* environment — their own editor, their own shortcuts, their own way of reading documentation — not a stripped-down browser code box that doesn't resemble how anyone actually writes software day to day.

## The case for watching, not co-editing

A shared coding sandbox is good for a specific kind of exercise. It's a worse fit when the role is about debugging a real, messy codebase, reasoning about a system design on a whiteboard app, or walking through an existing project the candidate brought with them. In those cases, you want to watch them work, ask questions, and let them drive — and a sandboxed shared editor actively gets in the way of that.

## Setting up the interview

1. Decide in advance who's sharing — usually the candidate, since you want to see their setup and problem-solving process.
2. A few minutes before the interview, send the candidate the [caster link](/caster) so they aren't fumbling with a new tool the moment the call starts.
3. At the start of the interview, they click **Start sharing my screen** and give you the 4-digit code.
4. You open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver), enter the code, and watch on a second screen or window alongside your video call for audio.

No interview platform to onboard them into, no plugin, no account setup eating into a 45-minute slot. If you're interviewing with a co-interviewer, the same code supports both of you watching at once, so you don't need to relay what's happening on a second call.

## Things worth setting up beforehand

- Send the candidate the [receiver link](/receiver) in advance if *they'll* be watching something from you (a system diagram, a codebase to discuss), or the [caster link](/caster) if they'll be sharing.
- Confirm audio happens over your usual call tool — QuiiCast handles the screen, not the conversation, so you'll still need Zoom, Meet, or a phone call running alongside it.
- If the candidate is on a slower connection, suggest they pick a lower quality preset before starting; a crisp 480p feed of a terminal is far more useful than a stuttering 4K one, since you're reading text, not watching video.
- Ask the candidate to close anything unrelated before sharing — personal tabs, other applications — both for their comfort and so you're only seeing what's relevant.

## FAQ

**Can two interviewers watch the same session?**
Yes — up to 5 people can join a single code, so a panel interview works the same way as a one-on-one, without a separate video call needed just to relay what's on screen.

**What if the candidate's screen share cuts out mid-interview?**
They can simply start sharing again from the caster page and send you a new code — it takes a few seconds and doesn't require rejoining any platform or re-authenticating anything.

**Does this work if the candidate is on a company-locked laptop with restricted software installs?**
Yes — since nothing needs to be installed, a locked-down or newly issued laptop with no admin rights still works fine, which is often not true of dedicated interview platforms that need a desktop client.

It's a small piece of a good interview process, not a replacement for one — but for "let me just watch you work," it removes a surprising amount of setup friction on both sides.
