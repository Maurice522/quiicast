Sometimes a recorded walkthrough video isn't what you need — you need a friend to actually watch you attempt the boss fight, tell you what you're doing wrong in real time, or just be there while you figure it out together. A video was recorded by someone with a different loadout, a different build, a different version of the game; a friend watching *your* actual attempt can tell you what's actually going wrong.

## Streaming platforms are built for a different thing

Full streaming setups are great for broadcasting to an audience, with chat, overlays, and archived VODs. That's a lot of setup — a streaming account, a stream key, OBS configured correctly — for showing one friend a single tricky section of a game for ten minutes.

## Getting a session running

1. Start sharing from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) — pick your game window, or your entire screen if the game runs in exclusive fullscreen mode.
2. Make sure your browser's share picker includes system/tab audio if you want your friend to hear the game, not just see it.
3. Send your friend the 4-digit code however you're already talking — Discord, text, whatever's fastest.
4. They open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) and are watching within seconds — on their laptop, or even their phone if they're not near a computer.

No streaming account, no stream key, no setting up OBS for a one-off session. Since up to 5 people can join the same code, it works just as well for a small group figuring out a co-op puzzle together as it does for one friend backseat-gaming a hard boss fight.

## Where this is genuinely useful

- Getting live advice on a section you're stuck on, instead of pausing to describe it in chat
- Showing a friend a glitch, an easter egg, or something ridiculous that happened, immediately
- Letting someone without the game watch you play through a story moment together
- Quick co-op strategy sessions where everyone needs to see the same map or inventory screen
- Settling an argument about whether a strategy actually works, by just showing it

## Getting the best picture for fast-paced games

Turn on **Prefer local WiFi** if you're both on the same network (like a LAN party), and pick a higher frame-rate quality preset for fast-paced games — 60fps makes a real difference for anything with quick motion, more so than for a mostly-static desktop share. If your friend's connection is choppy, dropping the resolution one notch usually preserves smoothness better than dropping the frame rate does.

## FAQ

**Will my friend hear the game audio?**
Yes, as long as you include system or tab audio when your browser prompts you during the share setup — this is a browser-level checkbox, not something QuiiCast needs to be told separately.

**Can my friend control anything, or just watch?**
Just watch. QuiiCast streams video and audio one-way; there's no remote-control feature, so it's for advice and reactions, not someone else pressing your buttons.

**Does this work for console gaming, like a PlayStation or Xbox?**
Only if the video is going through a device with a browser first — QuiiCast shares whatever a browser tab can capture, which means the source needs to be a computer, not a console plugged straight into a TV.

Whether it's advice on a boss fight or just wanting someone to witness a ridiculous in-game moment as it happens, this beats trying to describe it after the fact.
