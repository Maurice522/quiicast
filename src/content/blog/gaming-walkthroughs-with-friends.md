Sometimes a recorded walkthrough isn't what you need — you need a friend to actually watch you attempt the boss fight and tell you what you're doing wrong in real time. A video was recorded by someone with a different build or a different version of the game; a friend watching *your* actual attempt can tell you what's actually going wrong, right now.

## Why a full streaming setup is the wrong tool here

Streaming platforms are built for broadcasting to an audience, with chat, overlays, and archived VODs — a stream key and OBS configured correctly, for showing one friend a single tricky section of a game for ten minutes. Start sharing from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) instead, and your friend is watching within seconds on [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) — on a laptop, or their phone if they're not near a computer. No streaming account, no stream key. Full connection steps are on the [how it works](/how-it-works) page; the one game-specific thing worth doing before you start is checking your browser's share picker includes system or tab audio, so your friend hears the game and not just sees it.

![The real caster screen mid-session, with the code to send a friend](/blog/screenshot-caster-sharing.png)

Since up to 5 people can join the same code, this works just as well for a small group figuring out a co-op puzzle together as it does for one friend backseat-gaming a hard boss fight — getting live advice on a stuck section, showing off a glitch the moment it happens, or settling an argument about whether a strategy actually works by just showing it.

## Getting the best picture for fast-paced games

Turn on **Prefer local WiFi** if you're both on the same network (a LAN party, say), and pick a higher frame-rate quality preset for anything with quick motion — 60fps makes a real difference here, more so than for a mostly-static desktop share. If a friend's connection is choppy, dropping resolution one notch usually preserves smoothness better than dropping frame rate does.

## Discord screen share is the obvious alternative — here's the actual difference

![A friend's screen once connected and watching live](/blog/screenshot-receiver-watching.png)

If you and your friend are already in a Discord call, Discord's own screen share is genuinely the simpler choice, since audio and video are already in the same app. QuiiCast's edge is for the cases Discord doesn't cover well: a viewer who isn't in your Discord server, someone on a restrictive network where Discord's screen share quality drops badly, or just wanting a link that opens instantly without anyone needing a Discord account at all. For a regular gaming group already living in Discord, stick with what you have — this is for the one-off "someone not in our server needs to see this" moment.

## FAQ

**Will my friend hear game audio?** Yes, as long as you include system or tab audio when your browser prompts you during setup — a browser-level checkbox, not something QuiiCast needs told separately.

**Can my friend control anything, or just watch?** Just watch — QuiiCast streams one-way, so it's for advice and reactions, not someone else pressing your buttons.

**Does this work for console gaming, like a PlayStation or Xbox?** Only if the video passes through a device with a browser first — the source needs to be a computer, not a console plugged straight into a TV.

Whether it's advice on a boss fight or wanting someone to witness a ridiculous in-game moment as it happens, [start a cast](/caster) and send the code.
