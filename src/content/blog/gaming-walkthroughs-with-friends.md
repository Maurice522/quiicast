Sometimes a recorded walkthrough video isn't what you need — you need a friend to actually watch you attempt the boss fight, tell you what you're doing wrong in real time, or just be there while you figure it out together.

## Streaming platforms are built for a different thing

Full streaming setups are great for broadcasting to an audience, with chat, overlays, and archived VODs. That's a lot of setup for showing one friend a single tricky section of a game for ten minutes.

## How QuiiCast fits

Start sharing from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster), send your friend the 4-digit code, and they're watching on [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) within seconds — on their laptop, or even their phone if they're not near a computer. No streaming account, no stream key, no setting up OBS for a one-off session.

Since up to 5 people can join the same code, it works just as well for a small group figuring out a co-op puzzle together as it does for one friend backseat-gaming a hard boss fight.

## Where this is genuinely useful

- Getting live advice on a section you're stuck on, instead of pausing to describe it in chat
- Showing a friend a glitch, an easter egg, or something ridiculous that happened, immediately
- Letting someone without the game watch you play through a story moment together
- Quick co-op strategy sessions where everyone needs to see the same map or inventory screen

Turn on **Prefer local WiFi** if you're both on the same network (like a LAN party), and pick a higher frame-rate quality preset for fast-paced games — 60fps makes a real difference for anything with quick motion, more so than for a mostly-static desktop share.
