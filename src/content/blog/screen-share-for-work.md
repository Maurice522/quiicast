A lot of work happens between the meetings that are actually on the calendar. Someone messages "can you show me right now," a client wants a quick look at progress, or three people at a desk need to see the same spreadsheet for two minutes. None of that needs a scheduled call — it needs a screen and a code.

## Quick product demos

Some demos are scheduled a week out with a deck. Others happen because a prospect asked to see the thing you're describing mid-conversation, and the moment usually passes if you make them wait for a meeting link to spin up. Share your screen from [quiicast.com/caster](/caster), send the 4-digit code over whatever channel you're already talking in, and the prospect is watching within seconds on any device — no account, no app to install. This is deliberately not built for a formal, recorded, multi-stakeholder pitch; for that, a real video-conferencing tool is still the right choice.

## Walking someone through a document

Sending a document link works when the other person just needs to read or edit it on their own time. It's a worse fit when you need to explain *why* a formula is built a certain way before they touch it, or the document is sensitive enough that you don't want to grant edit access at all — sharing the screen shows exactly what's there, cursor and comments included, without a shared-drive invite or an account on whatever platform the file lives in.

## Showing a client work in progress

Giving a client staging credentials or a shared design-tool seat is reasonable for an ongoing engagement, and a lot of setup for "can I quickly show you where this is at." Share your screen with the work already open, and the client watches live, asking questions as they come up — no login to your tools, no risk of them poking around a staging environment and seeing something you didn't mean to show yet. A privacy note worth taking seriously here: close other client files and tabs first, since a stray notification or the wrong file name showing up mid-call looks unprofessional fast.

## Presenting to a small room

Not every presentation happens in a room with a projector already wired up. Four people around a desk, or a small team in a coworking space, can each open [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on their own laptop or phone and follow along on their own screen — up to 5 devices per code — which is often faster than finding the right HDMI adapter. If a couple of attendees are remote, they join with the same code from wherever they are.

## When a real video call is still the better tool

Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams solve a different problem: multiple people talking, camera feeds, breakout rooms, calendar integration, recording. QuiiCast doesn't do any of that — it's one screen, shown to whoever has the code, over audio you're already running separately. For a formal meeting with several stakeholders who need to be seen and heard, use the video platform your team already has. Reach for QuiiCast specifically for the moments those tools are overkill for: the two-minute "let me just show you," where opening a full meeting would take longer than the thing you're demonstrating.

## FAQ

**Does the other person need an account?** No — on either side. A browser and the 4-digit code are the only requirements.

**Can I record a demo for later?** Not through QuiiCast — nothing is recorded or stored server-side. For a recording, run separate screen-recording software locally.

**Can I share just one window instead of my whole screen?** Yes — most browsers let you choose a specific window or tab in the share picker instead of "Entire Screen," which is the safer default whenever other client work is open elsewhere on your desktop.

For the ad hoc conversations that would otherwise not happen at all because setting up a call felt like too much friction, [start sharing](/caster) and send the code.
