Not every presentation happens in a conference room with a projector already wired up. Sometimes it's four people around a desk, or a small team spread across a coworking space, and the fastest way to share your screen is whatever's already in everyone's pocket.

## When the meeting room tech is the bottleneck

Hunting for the right HDMI adapter, fighting with a projector's input source, or setting up a video call just so people three feet away can see your laptop screen is friction that has nothing to do with the actual presentation. It's especially frustrating in a borrowed or unfamiliar space — a client's office, a coworking space, a hotel meeting room — where you don't know the AV setup and don't have time to figure it out.

## Getting everyone looking at the same screen

1. Open [quiicast.com/caster](/caster) on your laptop and start sharing whatever you're presenting.
2. Put the 4-digit code on a sticky note, in the meeting invite, or just say it out loud to the room.
3. Everyone opens [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver) on their own laptop or phone and types the code.
4. Each person is watching on their own screen within seconds — up to 5 people on one code, which comfortably covers a small team meeting.

If everyone's on the same office WiFi, turning on **Prefer local WiFi** keeps the video traffic on the local network, which tends to mean lower latency for a room full of people watching the same feed.

## Where this beats a projector

- Small rooms where a projector is either unavailable or genuinely unnecessary
- Reviewing a design or document at a size everyone can actually read on their own screen, rather than squinting at a shared one across the room
- Hybrid-ish setups where a couple of people are remote but most are in person — remote folks join with the same code from wherever they are
- Impromptu walkthroughs that don't justify booking the one room with AV equipment
- Presenting in an unfamiliar space (a client's office, a rented room) where you have no idea what AV equipment is actually available

## A tip for the room

Suggest people use **Full screen** on their own device so the shared material fills their view without other browser tabs distracting from it — especially useful for anyone watching on a phone, where screen space is already limited.

## FAQ

**Does everyone need to be on the same WiFi?**
No — QuiiCast works over the regular internet by default, so remote attendees can join from anywhere. "Prefer local WiFi" is an optional setting for when everyone actually is on the same network, for a smoother connection.

**What if someone joins late?**
They just need the current 4-digit code — if it's changed since the meeting started (codes refresh automatically after a while), ask the presenter to read out the latest one.

**Is there a limit to how many people can watch?**
Up to 5 devices can join a single session at once, which covers the typical small-group scenario this is built for. For a larger audience, a proper presentation or webinar tool is a better fit.

It's not meant to replace a proper video-conferencing setup for a large, formal presentation — but for a handful of people who just need to see the same screen, it's usually faster than finding the right cable.
