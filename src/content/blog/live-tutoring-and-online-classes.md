Good tutoring is visual. Walking through a math proof, annotating an essay, or stepping through a line of code is a lot easier to follow when the student can actually watch it happen instead of imagining it from a description over the phone.

## Why dedicated classroom software is often overkill

Full e-learning platforms are great for scheduled classes with rosters and recordings, but for a one-off session — a single student, a single hour, a single topic — signing both people up for an account just to share a whiteboard is a lot of setup for not much payoff.

## How QuiiCast fits

A tutor opens [quiicast.com/caster](/caster), starts sharing their screen — a PDF, an IDE, a browser tab with practice problems — and sends the student the 4-digit code. The student opens [quiicast.com/receiver](/receiver), enters the code, and is watching within seconds, on their laptop, tablet, or phone.

Because it works in any modern browser, it doesn't matter what device the student has. And because there's no account on either side, there's no "I forgot my password" derailing the first five minutes of a session.

## Making the most of it

- Use **Full screen** on the student's end so the shared material fills their screen without your browser chrome around it as a distraction.
- If you're annotating or writing live, a slightly lower frame-rate quality preset is usually indistinguishable for handwriting or text, and it's easier on a student's connection if they're on mobile data.
- For a small study pod rather than 1:1, the same code supports up to 5 students watching at once — useful for a quick group review session before a test.

It won't replace a full classroom platform for a term-long course, but for a single focused session, it's the fastest way to get two screens looking at the same thing.
